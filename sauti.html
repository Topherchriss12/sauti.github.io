01The station

A station is a promise that someone will be there.

Not a folder of audio files. A station has a shape: the breakfast show, the request hour, the voice that says goodnight. Sauti is built around that shape and the people who make it happen.

Sauti runs the ordinary hours without asking for attention. It knows when to get out of the way. That is most of the work of running a station. The rest belongs to you.

Your library, your playlists, your presenters and your weekly grid live in one place. No encoder settings to remember before breakfast. No spreadsheet holding the schedule together. No engineer on call for the quiet hours.

The result is a control room small enough to learn in an afternoon, and reliable enough to leave on air when no one is in the building. Sauti is a station that runs itself, until you take the mic.

03The desk

When the mic opens, everything else gets out of the way.

Going live feels like stepping into a show, you never wrestle the software. Presenters use their own credentials with BUTT or any standard Icecast source client. Sauti takes over in about two seconds.

When the encoder closes, the station does not wait for someone to notice. The schedule resumes by itself, and the play log records the handover.

Live is a handover, not a takeover. The presenter’s credentials are issued for the duration of their show. When the desk closes, the key is revoked. No one else can use it.

  • ~2 secfrom authenticated audio to live
  • 1 keyper presenter, revocable
  • StandardIcecast source client

One handover / 15:00–15:47 EAT

  1. 15:00 The schedule is on air

    The Long Way Home plays from the weekly grid. Automation is holding the station.

  2. 15:02 Amina opens the encoder

    Live preempts automation. No one in the studio has to change a setting.

  3. 15:47 Amina closes the encoder

    Sauti sees the disconnect and hands the station back to the schedule.

  4. 15:47 The log keeps the record

    Live start, live end, and every track around it are written to the play log.

A connection card, not the keys to the building.

Credentials are issued per presenter. The live page gives each person exactly what to enter and nothing else. Give a key to the Friday host. Take it back when their show ends.

04Fallbacks

Silence is not a fallback.

Sauti keeps the next minutes (of your choice) playout planned. If a file, a schedule or a connection lets you down, the station climbs down a ladder until it finds something it can trust.

20 minutes of planned playout ahead

Enough room for a restart, a slow disk or a control-room hiccup to happen off air. When the horizon is running out, the safety layers take over.

Before you apply

A good fit if you

  • have a weekly rhythm, not just a one-off stream
  • are run by a small team, not a department
  • want live shows without a studio engineer
  • can bring the rights and permissions for your audio
  • can commit to a season of feedback and calls

Not yet if you need

  • ad scheduling, billing or subscriptions
  • browser go-live or a mobile presenter app
  • HLS, a CDN or a licensed FM transmitter
  • audience analytics beyond listener counts

Ask a question
What is Sauti?

A web-based control room for an internet radio station. It handles your library, weekly schedule, automated playout, live presenter takeovers, and the public stream.

Do listeners need an app?

No. Each station gets a public listener page with a play button. It works in any modern browser on a phone, tablet or computer, with no account. The stream is MP3, so it also plays in standard radio and audio apps (VLC, Music Player etc) if you have the stream URL.

What do I need to go live?

A computer, a microphone, and a standard Icecast source client such as BUTT. Sauti gives each presenter their own connection details. Browser-based go-live is not part of the pilot.

Can I schedule in my own time zone?

Yes. The weekly grid is set per station and shown in the station’s time zone. The default is Africa/Nairobi.

What happens when a live show ends?

Sauti detects the disconnect and hands the station back to the weekly schedule. The play log records the live block and the tracks around it. No one has to remember to press play.

What if something fails?

Sauti by default keeps about twenty minutes of playout planned. If the plan cannot continue, it moves through the station default playlist, a safety rotation, and finally an emergency bed. The goal is no listener visible silence while the control room recovers.

Can I play any music I like?

Only if you have the rights to broadcast it. You are responsible for permissions and any public performance royalties. Sauti does not supply music licences or report royalties to collecting societies during the pilot. Start with your own recordings, music you have licensed, or appropriately licensed Creative Commons work.

How much does it cost, and when can I join?

The first version is in a five station pilot. Pricing is not published yet, and there is no self-service signup. If you are invited, we agree the terms in writing before setup. Tell us about your station if you would like one of the places.
